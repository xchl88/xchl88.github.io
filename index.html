Credit

TeamSystem €700m 6.50% Senior Secured Notes due 2032

Public-information caseShadow credit memoAs of 23 June 2026
Question
Approve the notes, and at what yield?
The call
Approve

Comps put fair value at 7.00% (429bp). The notes priced at 6.50% (379bp over Bunds), about 50bp tight, which a B2/B/B software credit with 90% recurring revenue can carry.

Context
Italy's leading accounting, tax, payroll and ERP software provider, controlled by Hellman & Friedman since 2021 (69%). The 6NC2 notes redeemed the €300m 2028 notes, repaid the RCF and prefunded about €178m of earn-outs.

What drove the call

  • Addbacks make up 13–18% of marketed EBITDA, so I underwrote on statutory EBITDA of €527m, not €604m.
  • Cash covers interest about 2.8x but funds little debt reduction: LTM free cash flow was €122m against €566m of M&A and earn-outs, and the €650m HoldCo PIK would cost about €52m a year if paid in cash.
  • Liquidity (€834m, no maturity before January 2031) survives every case I ran, but leverage does not. In the severe case (EBITDA −43%), leverage peaks at 13.6x with interest cover of 1.6x.

Why it matters for portfolio lending

A fund-level lender sits above structures like this one. The marketed 4.70x left out €963m of HoldCo PIK and earn-out claims that rank ahead of the sponsor's equity, and that equity is the collateral behind a NAV loan. It is geared to the multiple: on adjusted net debt of €3,804m, equity is 52% of enterprise value at 15x statutory EBITDA and 28% at 10x, so a five-turn fall removes about 64% of its value.

Value

Beatrice Offshore Wind, 17.5% stake at £155m

Public-information caseShadow IC memoAs of 15 June 2026
Question
Should co-owner Equitix match £155m under its pre-emption right?
The call
Do not match

On public evidence the stake is worth £133.1m, so the price is 16.5% above value and returns 4.9%. Match only if four diligence gates clear: P50 of at least 2,300 GWh, life to 2054, a 15% post-subsidy cost cut and merchant prices 10% above TRIG's curve.

Context
TRIG agreed to sell its 17.5% of Beatrice (588 MW, Outer Moray Firth) for about £155m, 4% below its 31 December 2025 carrying value. Equitix exercised its pre-emption right; the memo tests that decision.

What drove the call

  • The value is the last eight years of the CfD at £205.70/MWh. After 2034, cash costs of about £174k/MW absorb most merchant revenue.
  • Output is the swing factor: each 1% of P50 is worth about £4.7m. I rebuilt P50 at 2,300 GWh from a 30-year ERA5 wind index (R² 0.90), correcting for a less windy period and a 2025 cable fault. It is flagged as an estimate, not a yield study.
  • No single assumption reaches the price. Only the three buyer levers together get there, at £154.5m and a 9.2% IRR, with no margin of safety.

Why it matters for portfolio lending

Every NAV mark raises the same question: what would a buyer pay? Here private buyers paid close to NAV while listed funds holding similar cash flows traded 23–32% below it. TRIG sold Beatrice 4% below NAV while buying back its own shares at about 35% below.

Portfolio

Global equity dependence at two horizons

Academic researchUCL MSc dissertationAugust 2026
Question
Does sampling daily or weekly change the dependence a risk model measures, and the risk a portfolio then realises?
Finding
Horizon matters

Measured dependence changes with the sampling horizon, mostly in pairs of markets that close at different times. Whether that changes realised risk depends on the portfolio's constraints.

Data
Fourteen equity markets across the Americas, EMEA and Asia-Pacific, 2005–2025, from LSEG: 3,579 common trading days, 1,056 weeks, 91 pairs. Five questions were registered before estimation.

+0.017

What the tests show

  • Closing-time separation tracks the gap and geographic distance does not: +0.017 per hour (permutation p = 0.001), against t = 0.35 for distance once both are in the regression.
  • A 10% position cap changes the comparison by +0.59 points (95% CI +0.15 to +1.13). Constraints make covariance structure matter, although the capped difference itself is not distinguishable from zero.
  • Removing the first common component reduces connectedness by 45–51% daily and 65–69% weekly.

Why it matters for portfolio lending

In listed markets, daily data show the lowest dependence for markets whose prices are recorded furthest apart in time, and the gap to weekly data grows with that separation. Private holdings are marked quarterly and their valuations are smoothed, a related problem known as stale pricing, which this study does not test. For a NAV portfolio, the implication is to read diversification from quarterly marks with caution and to stress holdings together rather than one at a time.

How the work is built

Dated. Each case has an as-of date. Where a later filing stands in for data investors had at that date, the page says so: TeamSystem's pro forma figures come from the H1 2026 report as a proxy for the offering memorandum, cross-checked against the Q1 report available at pricing.

Two bases. Company-reported figures and my adjustments sit side by side and are never merged, as in the TeamSystem leverage bridge above.

Sourced. Every hard-coded input points to a named document. Inputs without one are flagged as assumptions, such as the TeamSystem recovery multiple and the Beatrice P50.

Rebuilt. Models are generated from scripts and recalculated independently before any figure reaches a memo.

Sized to the evidence. Where a test does not reject, the text says so and goes no further. The dissertation reports a constraint-dependent change, not a measured cost.

Decision first. Each memo opens with the recommendation and the few numbers that carry it, before the detail.
